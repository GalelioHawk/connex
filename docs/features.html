Features

Four jobs.
One icon on your home screen.

Connex replaces the app-hopping of an ordinary day — the messenger, the alert group, the study folder and the emergency plan — with one place that does all of it.

01 — Chat

Messaging that carries the day

Everything you expect from a messenger, with the details done right — including how it behaves when the network isn't perfect.

  • 1:1 and group chats — groups with names, photos, members and admin controls.
  • Voice & video calls — with a call history tab next to your conversations.
  • Status updates — photo, video or text updates that expire after 24 hours.
  • Sent · delivered · read — tick marks on every message, with read receipts you can switch off.
  • Privacy controls — decide who sees your photo, your last seen and your online status.

02 — Feed & Alerts

The neighbourhood, in real time

The Feed is where your community posts. The Alerts tab is where the important part rises to the top — official data and neighbour reports, sorted into six categories and filtered to your area.

  • Loadshedding schedules — official stage data refreshed automatically through the day.
  • Community tagging — anyone can tag a post as Utility, Safety, Traffic, Water, Weather or Community.
  • Area filtering — set your area on your profile; alerts from elsewhere stay out of your way.
  • A normal feed too — likes, comments and photos for the everyday posts in between.

03 — Edu

Learning support, starting with tutoring

Connex Edu is being built as a South African learner support hub: tutor requests first, then question-paper downloads and notes requests around the same learner profile.

  • Tutor requests — learners or parents can ask for help by grade, subject, mode and budget.
  • Tutor approval — tutor profiles and reviews will appear only after real onboarding, approval and learner bookings.
  • Question papers — past papers and memos organised by grade, subject, year and session.
  • Notes requests — learners can ask for notes by subject and topic, then save approved notes as the library grows.

04 — SOS

Designed for the worst minute of the week

SOS lives inside the chat tab, always one hold away. It exists for the moment when dialling, unlocking and explaining are all too slow.

1
Mutual-consent contacts

You request, they accept. Nobody becomes your emergency contact without agreeing to it.

2
Three-second hold

A countdown with haptic pulses — long enough to prevent accidents, short enough to matter.

3
Emergency alert

Every accepted contact receives an emergency message and push notification. Live location is planned for a later phase.
